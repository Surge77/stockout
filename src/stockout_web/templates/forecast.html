Retail sales and demand

Pick a {{ entity_label }} and a day. The answer is two predictions from two different models: how much it will sell, and whether that is a Low, Medium or High demand day for that {{ entity_label }}.

What am I actually asking for?

The models read a date as a kind of day — which weekday, which month, which week of the year — and answer from the {{ date_min[:4] }}–{{ observed_through[:4] }} sales history they were fitted on. No feature is a lag, so nothing about a day has to have happened yet for the models to score it, and the date box is open-ended forward.

Two regions, and they mean different things. Up to {{ observed_through }} the real figure is in the data, so the page shows it beside the prediction and you can see the error — the model being checked rather than trusted. Past that date there is nothing to check against: the answer is a genuine forecast, and no measured score covers it.

Both answers are relative to the {{ entity_label }} you picked, not to all {{ entity_label }}s. A big shop and a small one have their own idea of a busy day, so “High” always means high for this shop.

The two models are fitted separately and are allowed to disagree — when they do, the page shows both rather than hiding one.

Ordered quietest to busiest, so the list reads as a range. The figure is that shop's median day across {{ date_min }}–{{ observed_through }}.
Data runs to {{ observed_through }}; up to there the true figure is shown beside the prediction. Later dates are answered as forecasts. Earlier than {{ date_min }} is refused — there is nothing behind it.
